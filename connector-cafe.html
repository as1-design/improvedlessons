Step 1 Linkers connect ideas and show how they are related: one idea can be the result, the reason, the purpose or a contrast with another. Every linker on this page also belongs to one of two families, and the family decides where the linker goes in the sentence and how the sentence is punctuated. Read the key words below, then read about both families before you continue.

Words used on this page

clause
A group of words with a subject and a verb: the traffic was heavy, she is eighty-five.
noun phrase
A noun together with the words around it, but without a verb: the heavy traffic, a water leak.
-ing form
A verb ending in -ing and used like a noun: being tired, the traffic being so heavy.
infinitive
The base form of a verb: improve, catch, as in to improve, to catch.
modal verb
A verb such as can, could, will, would, may, might, used before another verb.
register
How formal or informal a piece of language is. A message to a friend and an official notice use different registers.

Family 1 · Joiners

Joiners link two parts of the same sentence. Most of them are followed by a clause, but some are followed by a noun phrase or an -ing form, and some by an infinitive.

When the sentence starts with the linker, put a comma at the end of the first part: Although it was late, we stayed. / Because of the rain, we stayed in.
Before but, so and yet there is usually a comma: The meeting was OK, but the journey home was a nightmare. The comma can be left out when both parts of the sentence are short.
Before although and even though in the middle of a sentence, the comma is optional. Your book uses both forms.
Before because, so that, in case and to in the middle of a sentence, there is normally no comma.

Family 2 · Signposts

Signposts connect a new sentence to the sentence before it. They cannot join two clauses on their own.

Before a signpost, use a full stop or a semicolon, never only a comma. This is why however cannot replace but: It was expensive, but we bought it. / It was expensive. However, we bought it.
When a signpost starts a sentence, put a comma after it: However, it may be too expensive. / Therefore, we have decided to close.
Therefore and consequently can also go before the main verb. In that position they take no commas: …and therefore we are unable to offer you the job.

Step 3 The Bench

On each bench you will see the two halves of a sentence. Tap a linker to join them. The facts stay the same, but each linker changes something: the order of the two halves, the punctuation, the formality, or the form of the second half. Before each tap, try to predict the result. Will there be a comma or a full stop? Will the second half need to change? Then read the explanation under the sentence. To complete this step, try at least three linkers on each of the three benches.
